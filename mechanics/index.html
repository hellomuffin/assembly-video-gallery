What changed

  • Placing a rail does not create a rigid connection to the vertical part.
  • The screw advances through a modeled helical thread as torque turns it. Contact between the head and wood generates clamping force.
  • After tightening, the driver is off and the support is lowered. The connection must carry the rail’s weight on its own.
  • Contact stays enabled. Loosening changes the physics and allows motion again.

What these tests establish

  • All eleven furniture screw interfaces pass isolated load tests: eight rail joints with support removed, plus three top/step joints with an upward pull.
  • Without a screw, the rail drops about 24 cm.
  • With an inserted but unturned screw, the rail sags about 3.4 cm and has no head clamping force.
  • After tightening, the rail settles less than 1 mm and holds with no support contact or driver torque.
  • Reversing the screw releases clamping force and produces about 3.4 cm of sag.

All eleven furniture-joint results · Simplified-joint acceptance checks · Correction to the earlier full video

Scope: the six comparison clips are isolated joint bench tests. Two use the furniture CAD geometry; four use a simplified joint. The vertical member is fixed in a bench fixture. Screw threads use a reduced-order helical constraint and friction; driver torque is an actuator input. Thread cutting, wood deformation, tool-bit contact, and human hands are not resolved here. These tests establish causal fastening behavior, not a zero sim-to-real gap.
