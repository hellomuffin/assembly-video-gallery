How planning works
  • A programmed assembly sequence chooses the next part and operation.
  • The controller uses measured part poses to align the grasp and tool, pauses to clear arm collisions, and checks clamp force after tightening.
  • Loose joints trigger another tightening pass. Success requires releasing every hand and fixture and letting the stool settle under gravity.

The hands have 58 independent target coordinates in total: position, rotation and 23 wrist/finger joints per hand. Controllers turn these targets into forces. This is structured task planning, not a learned full-human policy.

Simulation scope and evidence

This is a complete mechanical replay using one unchanged physical model. Hands use assisted grasps, fixtures support the unfinished assembly, and threads and the powered driver use simplified models. Furniture gravity and collisions remain active. Passing these checks does not establish realistic full-human dynamics or a zero sim-to-real gap.

This replay checks clamp force before lifting, physically retightens loose joints, and checks every screw again after release. The weakest final clamp is 10.07 N; no acceptance thresholds were relaxed.
