What the sequence includes

  • Loose components picked up from the floor.
  • Visible hands supporting and aligning each part.
  • A driver placed at the connector, with clockwise tightening and ratchet return strokes.
  • A two-hand lift, upright placement, and final release.

What stays physically meaningful

  • Furniture moves through rigid-body simulation with gravity and contact.
  • Placement alone does not create a rigid connection.
  • Connections activate only after the declared fastening requirements.
  • Final checks verify released grasps, completed fasteners, and stable upright furniture.

The simplification follows your rule. Finger contacts and ratchet internals are assisted atomic operations. The hand position, rotation direction, fastening progress, and resulting connection still matter. The human surface and gait are kinematic animation, not a dynamically balanced human simulation.

How the same executor handles both furniture designs
  • Furniture descriptions supply panel geometry, mass, grasp locations, connector frames, and the final lifting grips.
  • Shared movement lifts the full held component or assembly above obstacles before translating and turning it.
  • Shared fastening checks alignment, tool position and axis, tightening direction, progress, and torque requirements.
  • Compact action control chooses the part or connector, target pose, grip state, and driver turn. Reusable handle grips, panel pinches, and arm IK generate the many visible hand/body coordinates; the planner does not search every finger joint independently.
  • A supplied correct task order drives these demonstrations. This is execution validation, not an evaluation of an assistant choosing the assembly order.

These are original furniture designs with explicit factory-installed brackets and captive screws. Broader CAD coverage and new connector mechanisms need their own descriptions and validation.

Checks, source, and precise scope
  • Both complete furniture tasks tested from two randomized layouts.
  • Recorded constraints checked against declared grasps and completed fasteners.
  • Sampled furniture penetration checked below 3 mm; sampled driver clearance checked against the panels.
  • Engaged tool-tip alignment checked within 5 mm; clockwise stroke direction and fastening timing checked.
  • Leg clearance checked against panels, mounting brackets and the driver rack, including intermediate motion samples. The rendered trouser mesh is checked at every recorded frame.
  • Final upright placement means alignment with gravity, not an exact world heading.
  • The core regression suite checks incomplete tightening, release, wrong tool placement or direction, and loosening.

Recording audits sample at 25 Hz. They do not certify every finger contact, human-body collision, fastener strength under overload, or a zero sim-to-real gap.
